# Research Standards

## Separate evidence from conclusions

For each meaningful claim, distinguish:

- **Documented fact:** what a cited source explicitly says. Attribute the statement to the source; do not imply it is automatically true.
- **Inference:** a conclusion supported by cited evidence and stated reasoning.
- **Hypothesis:** a plausible explanation that still needs testing.

Do not turn an index entry, family story, copied tree, or unsourced note into an established fact. Never invent a citation, source detail, quotation, or search result.

## Evaluate each source in context

Assess whether the source is original or derivative, who created it and why, how close the informant was to the event, whether the information is direct or indirect, and what errors or omissions may be present. Prefer the source best suited to the specific question; no source type is automatically conclusive.

Transcribe faithfully. Mark illegible text rather than guessing, distinguish a transcription from an interpretation, and retain enough location detail to find the image or record again. Note access restrictions and derivative copies.

## Cite reproducibly

Assign stable IDs such as `S-0001`. Record the creator, record or collection title, date, repository or website, collection and item identifiers, page or image, URL when applicable, access date, and exact location within the source. Include a short-form citation alongside each claim and link it to the full source record.

## Log searches, including failures

Record the question, repositories and databases searched, date and geographic coverage, spelling variants, filters, exact search terms where possible, search date, results, and known gaps. A failed search is evidence about that search only; it does not prove that a record or event never existed.

## Resolve conflicts transparently

List each conflicting statement with its source and source quality. Explain the reasoning for preferring one account, or leave the conflict unresolved. Do not silently harmonize dates, names, places, or relationships.

## Use calibrated confidence

- **High:** multiple relevant, sufficiently independent sources support the conclusion, conflicts have been addressed, and the reasoning is explicit.
- **Moderate:** the conclusion is supported, but meaningful limitations or unresolved alternatives remain.
- **Low:** evidence is sparse, indirect, derivative, or substantially conflicting.
- **Unresolved:** available evidence does not justify choosing among alternatives.

Confidence describes the current conclusion, not the certainty of an individual source. Revisit it when new evidence appears. Avoid numerical probabilities unless a defensible method is stated.
